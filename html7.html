Lesson 7 — Media

Estimated time: 30–45 minutes · Level: Beginner
Back to Lesson 6
Overview Audio Video Captions & transcripts Performance & lazy loading Accessibility Examples Sources

Overview

This lesson covers embedding and controlling audio and video, accessibility considerations (captions, transcripts), responsive media techniques, and performance best practices. Media enhances content but must be implemented with accessibility and performance in mind.

Audio

Use the <audio> element for sound. Provide controls for user playback and include fallback content. Prefer common formats (MP3, AAC, Ogg) and offer multiple sources when necessary.

<audio controls>
  <source src="audio/podcast-episode.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
  <source src="audio/podcast-episode.ogg" type="audio/ogg">
  Your browser does not support the audio element.
</audio>
Tip: Avoid autoplaying audio. Let users control playback to respect context and accessibility.

Video

Use the <video> element with controls. Provide multiple source formats and a poster image for the initial frame. For complex layouts, use the <picture> pattern for poster images if needed.

<video controls width="720" poster="images/hero-poster.jpg">
  <source src="video/lecture-720.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <source src="video/lecture-720.webm" type="video/webm">
  Your browser does not support the video element.
</video>
Adaptive delivery

Deliver multiple resolutions and let the player or CDN choose the best stream for the user's connection. Consider HLS/DASH for long-form or adaptive streaming.

Captions and transcripts

Always provide captions for video and transcripts for audio. Captions help deaf or hard-of-hearing users and improve comprehension for all users. Use <track kind="captions"> for captions and provide a downloadable transcript if possible.

<video controls>
  <source src="video/lecture.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <track kind="captions" srclang="en" label="English" src="captions/lecture-en.vtt">
</video>
Tip: Use WebVTT (.vtt) for captions. Ensure captions are synchronized and accurately reflect spoken content and important non-speech audio cues.

Performance and lazy loading

Media can be heavy. Use these techniques to reduce impact:

  • Lazy-load offscreen media (defer loading until user interaction or viewport).
  • Use optimized encodings and multiple resolutions.
  • Serve media from a CDN and enable range requests for efficient seeking.
  • Use the preload attribute carefully: metadata is often a good default.
<video controls preload="metadata">...</video>

<img loading="lazy" src="images/hero.jpg" alt="...">
Lazy initialization

Defer creating heavy players until the user clicks a play button; replace a lightweight poster with the actual <video> element on demand.

Accessibility

Accessible media includes captions, transcripts, descriptive text for important visuals, and keyboard-accessible controls. Use ARIA where native semantics are insufficient, and ensure focus management for custom players.

  • Provide captions (<track kind="captions">) and transcripts.
  • Offer keyboard controls and visible focus states for custom players.
  • Include descriptive alt text for poster images and meaningful title attributes when appropriate.
Practice: Test media with a screen reader and keyboard-only navigation to confirm controls and captions are usable.

Examples — practical snippets

Simple audio player with transcript

<audio controls>
  <source src="audio/interview.mp3" type="audio/mpeg">
</audio>

<section aria-labelledby="transcriptTitle">
  <h3 id="transcriptTitle">Transcript</h3>
  <p>[00:00] Host: Welcome...</p>
</section>

Video with captions and poster

<video controls width="640" poster="images/video-poster.jpg">
  <source src="video/intro.mp4" type="video/mp4">
  <track kind="captions" srclang="en" label="English" src="captions/intro-en.vtt">
</video>
Tip: Provide both captions and a transcript for maximum accessibility and searchability.

Summary

Use native media elements for straightforward playback, always provide captions and transcripts, optimize media for performance, and prioritize accessible controls. Thoughtful media implementation improves reach and user experience across devices and abilities.
